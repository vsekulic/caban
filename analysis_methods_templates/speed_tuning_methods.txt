SPEED-BINNED ROBUSTNESS ANALYSIS OF THE CELL-AVERAGED FIRING RATE
==================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
The whole-session spike-rate panels (sp_rates/) average over every cell and every frame, and the
navigation-aware panels (navigation_aware_single_cell/place_cell_rates/) split those frames with a
BINARY 2 cm/s movement threshold. The locomotion comparison
(navigation_aware_single_cell/locomotion_metrics/) shows that the viral groups do not differ on any
locomotion summary in any session -- distance travelled, mean speed, mean speed while moving, or
percentage of time moving.

Hippocampal firing is nevertheless GRADED with running speed. Two groups can have identical mean
speed while distributing their time across speeds differently, and a binary threshold cannot see
that. This analysis closes that gap.

WHY THE RATES ARE NOT SIMPLY DIVIDED BY SPEED OR DISTANCE
---------------------------------------------------------
Normalizing firing rate by speed or by distance travelled is not a hippocampal convention, and is
avoided here deliberately:

  * The firing-rate/speed relation has a substantial NONZERO INTERCEPT. A rate/speed ratio
    therefore diverges as speed approaches zero and is not interpretable.
  * It would break comparability with every published firing rate, which is reported in events or
    spikes per second.
  * The standard treatment of immobility in this literature is RESTRICTION, not normalization: a
    running-speed threshold (typically 2-5 cm/s) applied identically to spike counts and to
    occupancy. The rationale is physiological rather than statistical -- the hippocampus occupies
    two distinct network states, theta during locomotion and large-irregular-activity/sharp-wave-
    ripple during immobility, with qualitatively different firing statistics. Averaging across
    them mixes two phenomena; it is not a confound to be divided out.

The two approaches used here instead are the ones the literature uses when a manipulation may
affect behaviour: speed as a covariate, and reweighting to a common speed distribution
(direct standardization).

SPEED BINS
----------
Frames are assigned to fixed, global speed bins with edges (cm/s):

    0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, infinity

Three properties are deliberate:

  * The first internal edge is the movement threshold used everywhere else in the pipeline, so
    bin 0 is EXACTLY the existing "immobility_only" frame class and the union of bins 1+ is
    EXACTLY "movement_only". This is asserted by exact boolean equality for every mouse and
    session, and it is what makes this analysis nest inside the binary one rather than sit beside
    it.
  * Bins are not quantile-based. The speed distribution is heavily zero-inflated, so quantile bins
    would spend most of their resolution subdividing the sub-threshold tracking noise floor.
  * Sub-threshold speed is a single bin. Below 2 cm/s the signal is largely tracking jitter
    convolved with the ~200 ms smoothing kernel; subdividing it would measure the smoother rather
    than the animal. The top bin is open so no fast frames are discarded.

The speed used in every fit is the OCCUPANCY-WEIGHTED MEAN SPEED REALIZED within the bin, per
mouse, never the nominal bin centre: the top bin has no nominal centre, and within a wide bin a
nominal centre would inject a spurious group difference if one group habitually occupies the
bottom of a bin and another the top.

BIN RETENTION
-------------
A bin is retained for a session only if EVERY mouse in that session occupies it for at least 3 s.
Retention is common across mice rather than per-mouse: with a per-mouse rule the fastest point of
a group curve could rest on three animals of one group and one of another, which is a selection
artifact rather than a group comparison. The common rule makes the mouse-by-bin matrix complete by
construction, so the error band at every bin is over the same animals.

The retained set must be contiguous. Occupancy is not monotonic in speed in this data -- the
observed distributions are multi-modal, with local dips around 6-8 and 12-16 cm/s -- so contiguity
is a conservative structural guard rather than a monotonicity argument: an interior gap would mean
an animal barely samples one speed range while sampling both the slower and the faster ranges
around it, which warrants inspecting the velocity trace and is therefore treated as a hard error.
Truncation at the bottom is different and
is legitimate: on LT1 one animal (G06) never drops below 2 cm/s across the whole 612 s session
(minimum smoothed speed 2.06 cm/s), so the immobility bin is dropped for that session and LT1 is
analysed as a moving-bins-only session. This is the same animal and the same documented
behavioural property recorded in place_cell_restricted_rates_methods.txt.

Coverage -- the fraction of a bin set's natural universe that survives retention -- is computed
per mouse, reported in verification.txt and in the panel titles, and gated at 0.75. The
denominator is the universe the bin set is meant to cover (all frames for all_bins, all moving
frames for moving_bins), so that deliberately excluding immobility is not scored as lost data.

Retained bin counts differ between sessions (the chamber sessions are slower than the linear
track), so SLOPE MAGNITUDES ARE COMPARABLE ACROSS GROUPS WITHIN A SESSION, NEVER ACROSS SESSIONS.

TWO BIN SETS
------------
Everything below is computed twice: over all retained bins ("all_bins") and excluding the
immobility bin ("moving_bins"). Bin 0 contains freezing, and freezing is itself an effect of the
manipulation under study -- that is, a mediator. Standardizing over a bin set that includes it
partially adjusts away part of the effect of interest, so the mediator-free variant is always
reported alongside.

SIGNAL AND THE DEFINITION OF "AVG. SPIKE RATE"
----------------------------------------------
Deconvolved calcium event times (S), giving rates in events/s.

The quantity plotted on the tuning curves, and standardized in the section below, is defined for
each mouse and speed bin b as:

    rate_b = (total events across all cells of the class falling in bin b)
             / (n_cells * seconds of occupancy in bin b)

That is, it is the MEAN ACROSS CELLS of each cell's own event rate -- events per second per cell
-- not a summed count over cells. The denominator is cell-seconds of observation.

The figures label this "Avg. spike rate (events/s)". It is deliberately NOT called a "population
rate": that phrase reads as a summed quantity over all cells, and the two differ by a factor of
n_cells, which varies between animals.

Two properties of this definition matter for reading the figures:

  * SILENT CELLS REMAIN IN THE DENOMINATOR. A cell with no events in a given speed bin
    contributes 0 to the numerator and still counts as one cell. This is deliberate and is the
    same convention used by the whole-session sp_rates panels and by the movement-restricted
    panels (see the Computation section of place_cell_restricted_rates_methods.txt): it is what
    allows a silencing manipulation to appear as a reduced average rate rather than being
    divided away by a shrinking active-cell count.
  * IT IS THE SAME QUANTITY AS THE PANELS THIS ANALYSIS DEFENDS, restricted to a speed bin
    instead of to a time window or a binary movement class. That commensurability is the point;
    a robustness check denominated in a different quantity would not defend those panels. It is
    the identical convention implemented by get_avg_sp_rate_in_frame_mask() in caban/utilities.py,
    and the self-standardization identity check below relies on it algebraically: occupancy-
    weighting a mouse's own per-bin rates telescopes back to its raw rate only because the
    numerator and denominator are defined this way.

For the per-cell Poisson fits the same events are used without the across-cell averaging: each
cell's own counts and its own exposure (seconds, not cell-seconds) enter the model directly.

The Poisson likelihood below requires counts. Note that the
single-unit response analyses elsewhere in this pipeline default to the denoised trace C, for a
reason that does not transfer here: those tests contrast a ~10 s epoch against a ~10 s baseline,
where rare S events are too sparse to detect modulation, whereas each speed bin here pools tens to
hundreds of seconds.

MODEL: POISSON GLM WITH LOG LINK
---------------------------------
For each cell, and separately for each mouse's pooled cells, binned event counts are
regressed on realized speed:

    count_b ~ Poisson(mu_b),    log mu_b = alpha + beta * speed_b + log(exposure_b)

where exposure_b is seconds of occupancy in bin b (times the number of cells for the pooled
per-mouse fit).

The LOG LINK is essential to the interpretation and is not a fitting convenience. The claim this
analysis is designed to test is whether a group difference lives in the speed-independent
component of firing or in speed modulation. On an ADDITIVE scale that question cannot be answered:
a multiplicative gain change -- which is what a chemogenetic manipulation produces -- multiplies
slope and intercept by the same factor, so a purely cell-intrinsic effect would appear as a slope
difference and be misread as locomotion-driven. On the log scale, beta is the FRACTIONAL change in
rate per cm/s, and a pure gain change moves alpha alone.

The log-exposure offset weights each bin by how long the animal actually spent there. Ordinary
least squares on binned rates would instead treat a 3 s bin and a 400 s bin as equally
informative, which is the dominant noise source in a per-cell fit.

Fitting binned counts rather than per-frame samples is not an approximation: the binned-count GLM
with offset is the sufficient-statistic form of per-frame Poisson regression on a step function of
speed. It is also the only defensible choice here, because the velocity trace is upsampled from a
15 Hz behaviour camera to the 20 Hz imaging rate and Gaussian-smoothed (sigma = 4 frames, ~200
ms). Per-frame samples are consequently around 10x oversampled, and per-frame standard errors
would be badly anticonservative.

The estimator is verified on each run by recovering a known (alpha, beta) from synthetic Poisson
counts. This is the only check that catches a sign or scale error in the exposure offset, which
would otherwise produce entirely plausible-looking slopes.

TEST: GROUP x SPEED INTERACTION
--------------------------------
The primary test is a two-stage summary-statistic analysis, consistent with the rest of this
suite and free of pseudoreplication: one Poisson slope is fit per mouse, and the slopes are
compared across groups by one-way ANOVA gated on Tukey HSD, with n = mice. Comparing per-mouse
slopes across groups IS the group x speed interaction test.

A NULL INTERACTION IS THE DESIRED RESULT. It means the groups modulate their firing with speed
identically, so the whole-session rate difference cannot be a graded speed effect.

Because a null result from n = 5/6/6 animals is otherwise uninformative, three further quantities
are reported for every pairwise contrast in speed_tuning_group_stats.txt:

  * the 95% confidence interval (Welch) on the group difference in slope;
  * the fractional rate difference that a slope difference at each end of that interval would
    produce across the observed speed span, since on the log scale a slope difference d sustained
    over a span s multiplies the rate by exp(d*s);
  * the observed raw rate difference, so the two can be compared directly.

Together these convert "p > 0.05" into a bound: at the confidence limit, graded speed modulation
could account for at most a stated percentage of the observed rate difference.

A secondary, higher-powered analysis fits the slope per cell and compares the distributions with
a linear mixed model, slope ~ group + (1 | mouse), cell nested in mouse, mCherry as reference.
Cells enter this model only if they have at least 10 events spread over at least 2 retained bins.
Because a manipulation that silences cells would preferentially exclude that group's cells and
bias the surviving distribution, the per-group inclusion fraction is reported in the same file as
the model and must be read with it. The per-cell INTERCEPT distribution is deliberately not
plotted, as it would duplicate the existing cell-activity-distribution analysis.

Multiplicity: interaction tests across sessions and bin sets are additionally reported with
Benjamini-Hochberg FDR q-values. The rest of this suite does not correct for multiplicity, but
this is a null/robustness argument, where uncorrected multiplicity errs in the direction that
would destroy it -- a single spurious interaction among many tests would undermine the defence.

DIRECT STANDARDIZATION
----------------------
Each mouse's per-bin rates are reweighted to a common reference speed distribution:

    rate_standardized = sum_b w_b * rate_b

The reference w is the UNWEIGHTED MEAN ACROSS MICE of each animal's own occupancy proportions, not
the proportions of the pooled frames -- pooling would weight the reference by session length and
by how many animals each group happens to contribute. Every animal contributes equally to defining
the target distribution.

The estimand is the rate that would obtain if every animal distributed its time across speeds as
the reference animal does, within the speed range that every animal samples adequately.

Two references are reported: the all-mice mean, and the mCherry (control) mean, the latter being
the conventional epidemiological choice. If a conclusion differs between the two, it is not
robust, and reporting both makes that visible.

Because retention is common across mice, every mouse has a defined rate in every weighted bin by
construction; there is no missing-cell case to handle. Per-mouse bin gating with per-mouse
renormalization was rejected precisely because it would silently give each animal a different
estimand.

The SHIFT from the raw rate (rate_standardized - rate_raw, as a percentage) is reported as its own
panel and CSV column, not merely as a standardized value. Given that the groups do not differ in
locomotion, the correction is expected to be very small, and the magnitude of the correction is
the substantive result -- a violin of the standardized value alone would hide it.

SUPPORTING FIGURES
------------------
Speed-occupancy distributions. Per-mouse normalized occupancy across the retained bins, as a
per-group mean +/- SEM curve, plus a per-mouse total-variation distance from the reference
distribution compared across groups. This tests the premise of the whole analysis -- whether the
speed DISTRIBUTIONS differ and not merely their means, which the locomotion summaries cannot
address. It also licenses the standardization result: if the distributions superimpose, the
standardized rate must equal the raw rate.

Speed tuning curves. Cell-averaged rate against speed bin, per group, shown both in absolute terms
and with each mouse normalized by its own mean rate. The normalized panel separates height from
shape, which is the intercept/slope dissociation in visual form.

Cell-class tuning curves. The same curves computed separately for place and non-place cells, as a
positive control: place cells are expected to be the more strongly speed-modulated cell class, and
a failure of that expectation indicates a problem with the binning or the cell partition rather
than a biological result.

All error bands are the SEM ACROSS MICE at each bin, never across cells and never across frames.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
-----------------
Deconvolution latency. Deconvolved event times lag the underlying spikes by the calcium rise time
plus the deconvolution onset estimate, while the velocity smoothing kernel is symmetric. During
acceleration and deceleration this misalignment blurs the assignment of events to speed bins and
ATTENUATES the fitted slope -- that is, it biases towards the null result this analysis hopes to
find. The reported analysis uses zero lag; a sensitivity run shifting the speed trace by 4 frames
(200 ms) is available via the speed_lag_frames configuration option, and the structural assertion
tying bin 0 to the immobility frame class is necessarily skipped when a lag is applied. The
15 Hz to 20 Hz nearest-neighbour upsampling of the behaviour-camera trace adds a further ~67 ms of
timing quantization.

Statistical power. With 5, 6 and 6 animals per group, the interaction test is not well powered.
This is why the confidence intervals and their implied rate effects are reported, rather than the
p-value alone.

Scope. Whole-session window only. The 180 s pre-tone window used elsewhere in this suite leaves
too little time per moving speed bin to support this analysis, and the linear-track sessions have
no tones.
